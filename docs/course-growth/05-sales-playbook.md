# Sales Playbook

## Target profile
Independent public, municipal, and semi-private courses; smaller resorts; courses without a
dedicated internal marketing hire. Primary contacts: General Manager, Owner, Director of Golf,
Head Professional, Marketing Director (when one exists — usually doesn't at this tier).

## The wedge (never lead with "marketing")
Every script below opens with **"you already earned these golfers"** — never with "would you like
help with your digital marketing." The GM has heard that pitch a hundred times and has a form
rejection ready for it. "You've spent years building this list and these relationships — we help
you get more revenue out of them" is a different conversation because it's true, specific, and
doesn't ask them to admit their marketing is bad.

> "Hi, is this [First Name]? This is [Name] with Oregon Golf Guide — I work with golf courses

## 4. In-person introduction (pro shop counter / after a round)

> "Hi, I'm [Name] with Oregon Golf Guide — we work with a handful of Oregon courses on getting
> more revenue from the golfers already in their database. Is [GM/Director of Golf name] around,

Leave a one-page leave-behind (not a full brochure) — headline offer + guarantee + QR code to the
Apply page. Never pitch the full offer to front-desk staff; the goal is a warm handoff to the
decision-maker.

## 6. 15-minute discovery call structure

Goal: qualify + book the 30-minute sales call. Not a pitch.

1. **(2 min) Context** — confirm role, course type, how long they've been there.
2. **(5 min) Current state questions:**
   - "Do you have an email list today? Roughly how big, and where does it live?"
   - "How do you currently fill slow weekday or twilight tee times?"
   - "What's your membership/pass renewal process look like?"
   - "Anyone in-house currently sending emails or texts to golfers?"
3. **(4 min) Position the offer** — one sentence: "What we do is build a system that turns your
   existing list and traffic into more bookings and membership revenue, guaranteed to return at
   least 2x what you pay us. It's a 6-week buildout, then we run it every month."
4. **(3 min) Qualify + book** — if there's a real database or traffic to work with and a
   decision-maker on the line: "This sounds like a fit — let's do 30 minutes where I walk through
   exactly what we'd build for [Course Name] and the numbers. What's your calendar look like next
   week?"

Disqualifiers (politely end the call): no digital booking or POS data of any kind, course is
closing/for sale, no budget authority on the call and no path to the person who has it.

## 7. 30-minute sales call structure

1. **(3 min) Recap** — confirm what was learned on the discovery call.
2. **(5 min) The problem, in their words** — reflect back their specific leaks (e.g., "you said
   Tuesday/Wednesday tee times run half-empty most weeks, and your list hasn't been emailed in
   over a year").
3. **(7 min) The offer** — walk through the 6-week buildout and the four pillars, using their
   specific course as the example throughout (this is the 20%-customized moment in the sales
   process — it should not sound like a generic deck).
4. **(5 min) The guarantee** — explain the $2:$1 / 90-day guarantee in plain English (see script
   below), and the 90-day pilot offer.
5. **(5 min) Pricing** — $1,500 setup + $2,000/month, 90-day initial term. Say the number plainly,
   don't bury it.
6. **(5 min) Objections + next step** — handle whatever comes up (see objection library below),
   close on a next step: proposal sent within 24 hours, decision timeline confirmed.

### Guarantee sales explanation (verbal script)
> "Here's how the guarantee works: over your first 90 days live, we track every dollar of revenue
> we can directly tie to a campaign — promo codes, tracking links, bookings through the flows we
> build. If that trackable revenue doesn't add up to at least twice what you've paid us in fees by
> day 90, you stop paying the management fee until it does. The only conditions on our end are the
> normal stuff — we need access to your booking/POS system, reasonably quick turnaround on
> approvals, and you not pulling your available tee-time inventory out from under the campaigns
> we're running. Full terms are in the agreement, but that's the shape of it."

## 8. Proposal template

See `templates/proposal-template.md` for the fillable version. Structure:
1. Cover page — course name, date, prepared by
2. "What we heard" — 3–5 bullets reflecting their specific situation from discovery/sales calls
3. The offer — 6-week buildout + monthly management, four pillars
4. The guarantee — plain-English version
5. Pricing — setup fee, monthly fee, term
6. Timeline — week-by-week buildout schedule
7. What's needed from the course (access checklist)
8. Next steps + signature block

## 9. Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We're already busy." | "That's exactly why this works — it's done-for-you. We need about an hour of your time in week one for the audit call, then a few minutes a month to approve the calendar. We do the building and sending." |
| "We don't need marketing." | "This isn't marketing in the brand-awareness sense — it's a revenue system for the database and traffic you already have. Think of it less like advertising and more like a second person working your tee sheet and membership renewals full-time." |
| "We don't have the budget." | "Understood — quick way to think about it: it's about $65/day, and we guarantee at least double that back in trackable revenue within 90 days or you stop paying. Most courses find the risk is on our side, not yours." |
| "Someone internally handles email." | "Great — we're not trying to replace that relationship, we're building the system underneath it: the automations, the segmentation, the tracking. A lot of courses keep someone in-house for tone/local knowledge and let us run the infrastructure and the calendar." |
| "Our golfers already know us." | "Right — that's the whole point. We're not trying to introduce you to new golfers, we're trying to get more revenue out of the ones who already know you. Most courses are sitting on a list that hasn't been emailed properly in a year or more." |
| "We just post on Facebook." | "Social's fine for visibility, but it doesn't reliably drive booked revenue the way a direct email or text to your own list does — and you don't own that audience the way you own your list. We build the owned channel and prove the revenue." |
| "How do we know you generated the revenue?" | "Fair question — we use promo codes, dedicated tracking links, and your booking system's own data wherever possible, and we show you exactly how each number was tracked in the monthly report. If your system can't track something precisely, we say so and don't count it toward the guarantee." |
| "Golf is seasonal." | "Right — that's built into the calendar. We don't run the same campaign in December we run in June. The guarantee is measured over 90 days specifically because golf revenue isn't a straight line, and we don't think it's fair to hold either side to a single-month number." |
| "We don't want to discount tee times." | "We don't lead with discounting — the four pillars are about growing the list, bringing golfers back, and moving them to higher-value things like memberships and leagues. When we do target inventory that would otherwise go empty, it's about filling a tee time that earns zero dollars sitting empty, not about training your best customers to wait for a sale." |

## 10. 90-day pilot offer

Used to remove first-dollar friction for founding clients (first 5–10) and as a fallback close
when a prospect is interested but hesitant on a full-year mental commitment.

> **90-Day Founding Course Pilot**
> - Setup fee waived (normally $1,500)
> - $2,000/month for 3 months
> - Full $2:$1 guarantee applies, measured at day 90
> - If the guarantee isn't hit by day 90, management fees for that period are credited toward
>   month 4+ (not a cash refund — see plain-English terms in `08-guarantee-legal.md`)
> - No obligation to continue past day 90; if it worked, transition to standard month-to-month

This is a real discount (a waived setup fee, worth $1,500) bundled with the standing guarantee —
it is not a second, weaker guarantee. Sales team must not describe it as "extra risk-free" beyond
what the standard guarantee already provides.
