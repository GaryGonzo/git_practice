# The Guarantee — Marketing Copy, Sales Explanation, Plain-English Terms & Attorney Review Items

**This document distinguishes three different registers of the same guarantee. Do not blend them.
Marketing copy is persuasive and brief. Plain-English terms are the real substance and must be
linked from every place marketing copy appears. Contract language is a lawyer's job, not this
document's — see §4.**

---

## 1. Consumer-facing marketing language

Use on the website hero, in proposals, and in top-of-funnel outreach. Always paired with a visible
`*` and a link/reference to the plain-English terms (§3) — never presented with no qualification
in sight.

> **Make $2 for every $1 you spend with us — guaranteed.***
>
> *We track every dollar of revenue we can directly tie to a campaign. If it doesn't add up to at
> least double what you've paid us within 90 days of launch, you don't pay your management fee
> again until it does. Full terms and eligibility below.*

Short form (for space-constrained placements — signage, social, one-pagers):

> **2X your investment in 90 days — or the guarantee kicks in.***

---

## 2. Sales explanation (verbal, for calls)

> "Here's how it works: starting the day your system launches, we track every dollar of revenue we
> can directly tie to a campaign we ran — using promo codes, tracking links, or your booking
> system's own attribution where available. At the 90-day mark, we add it up. If that trackable
> revenue is at least double what you've paid us in fees over that period, the guarantee is met —
> which, honestly, is the expected outcome, not a stretch goal. If it's short, you stop paying the
> management fee and we keep working until the cumulative number clears the 2x bar. The guarantee
> has a few reasonable conditions on your side — we need access to your systems, timely approval
> turnaround, and you keeping your usual tee-time inventory available for the campaigns we run —
> all spelled out in the agreement, nothing hidden."

## 3. Plain-English guarantee terms (the real substance)

**What we guarantee:** At least $2.00 in trackable, campaign-attributable revenue for every $1.00
paid in management fees, measured cumulatively over the 90 calendar days following the account's
Week 6 launch date (the "Measurement Window").

**What counts as trackable, attributable revenue:** Revenue tied to a specific campaign via a
unique promo/booking code, a dedicated tracking link with booking-system or GA4 conversion
confirmation, or native revenue attribution inside the ESP/CRM platform. Directional/estimated
revenue (before-after comparisons where no direct tracking exists) does **not** count toward the
guarantee unless both parties agree in writing to include a specific instance.

**How the remedy works if the threshold isn't met:** Management fees for the next billing
period(s) are waived — not refunded in cash — until cumulative trackable revenue since launch
reaches the 2:1 threshold, up to a **maximum waiver period of 3 additional months.** After the
maximum waiver period, both parties revisit the plan together (adjust strategy, extend
measurement, or part ways) rather than the waiver continuing indefinitely.

**Eligibility requirements** (the course must, in good faith):
1. Provide and maintain the system access requested during onboarding (website, booking/POS,
   email/SMS platform).
2. Maintain reasonably available tee-time inventory for the campaigns being run — a course that
   pulls all discounted/twilight inventory or blocks the tee sheet during a campaign period isn't
   giving the campaign a chance to convert.
3. Respond to approval requests within the agreed SLA (2 business days).
4. Honor advertised offers and promo codes as run (no declining a valid promo code at the counter).
5. Keep tracking capabilities functioning — if the course changes booking/POS systems or removes
   analytics access mid-engagement without coordinating the transition, tracking gaps during that
   transition are excluded from the guarantee calculation.
6. Not materially change pricing, membership structure, or the specific offers being promoted
   without advance notice to us — a mid-campaign price change we didn't know about invalidates
   attribution for that specific campaign, not the whole window.
7. Follow the agreed strategy/calendar in good faith — the guarantee applies to the approved plan,
   not to a plan the course unilaterally altered or blocked mid-flight.

**What's excluded (force majeure / outside anyone's control):**
- Course closures due to weather, fire, natural disaster, or public health orders
- Golf-course-wide renovation, ownership change, or extended closure not caused by us
- Acts of god, extended power/internet/system outages at the course, or a booking/POS platform
  outage outside our control

**Guarantee clock pause:** If a covered exclusion event closes the course for more than 5
consecutive days during the Measurement Window, the window pauses for the duration of the closure
and resumes when the course reopens.

## 4. Items an attorney must specifically review before this goes into a contract

This document is a business/marketing framework, **not contract language.** Before any client
signs an agreement referencing this guarantee, have Oregon counsel review at minimum:

1. Whether "guarantee" language triggers any state advertising/consumer-protection disclosure
   requirements (Oregon UTPA and any FTC guidance on performance guarantees in B2B services).
2. Precise, enforceable definition of "trackable, attributable revenue" and dispute-resolution
   process if the parties disagree on what counts (recommend a named neutral review process or
   escalation path in the contract, not left informal).
3. Whether the remedy (fee waiver, capped at 3 months) is structured as a limitation of liability
   that will actually hold up, and whether a cash-refund alternative should ever be offered
   (recommend against it, but flag for counsel).
4. Proper force majeure clause drafting matched to Oregon contract law, not the plain-English
   version above.
5. Data ownership and access clauses — confirm the course's ownership of its own customer data
   and email/SMS list is unambiguous, including on termination.
6. SMS compliance language (TCPA, 10DLC carrier requirements) — ensure the contract properly
   allocates responsibility between course and agency for opt-in compliance.
7. Termination and transition terms — what happens to automations/campaigns/access at contract
   end, including within the 90-day minimum term.
8. Whether the "up to 2 rounds of revision" and other scope limits in the SOW need contractual
   (not just marketing-page) specificity to be enforceable as scope boundaries.
9. Confirm the guarantee's minimum-term structure (90 days) doesn't conflict with any Oregon
   rules on service-contract auto-renewal/cancellation notice periods.

**Do not publish final contract language, or represent the guarantee as legally binding in its
current plain-English form, until an attorney has reviewed and approved the actual service
agreement.**
