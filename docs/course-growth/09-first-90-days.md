# First 90 Days of Business Development & the Plan to Land the First 3 Oregon Clients

## Phase 1 — Days 1–14: Foundation
- Finalize legal entity/contract basics (attorney review of `08-guarantee-legal.md` items —
  start this immediately, it's the longest lead-time item)
- Stand up the internal tools: Google Workspace, Notion, Calendly, Stripe, a Klaviyo agency/demo
  account for showing prospects
- Publish the `packages/course-growth-site` website (this repo) live under the Oregon Golf Guide
  domain/subdomain
- Build the target list: 40–60 Oregon courses matching the target profile (independent public,
  municipal, semi-private, small resort — pull from NGF/Oregon Golf Association course directories
  and Discover Oregon Golf's own existing course data if available), with GM/Director of Golf
  contact info researched
- Draft and load the outreach sequence (cold email, follow-up, LinkedIn) into a simple tracking
  sheet (no need for a full CRM yet at this volume)

## Phase 2 — Days 15–45: Outreach & First Signed Client
- Begin outreach: target **10 new prospect contacts/week** across email, phone, and LinkedIn
  (per `05-sales-playbook.md`)
- Prioritize **warm-adjacent targets first**: courses already covered/featured on Discover Oregon
  Golf, courses within driving distance for an in-person introduction, any personal network
  connections
- Run every interested prospect through the 15-minute discovery call → 30-minute sales call
  sequence
- Lead with the **90-Day Founding Course Pilot** (setup fee waived) for the first 3–5 signed
  clients specifically to reduce first-dollar friction while the case-study library is still empty
- **Goal: first signed client by day 30, second by day 40–45**
- Start the buildout immediately on signature — don't stack a sales-only phase before any delivery
  begins; the first client's results are the foundation of the next 10 conversations

## Phase 3 — Days 46–75: Second & Third Client, Buildout Discipline
- Continue outreach at the same 10/week cadence — do not stop prospecting while delivering (a
  common solo-agency failure mode)
- **Goal: third signed client by day 60–70**
- Run the first client's 6-week buildout with strict adherence to the SOPs — the first buildout is
  also where the templates in `06-operations-and-sops.md` get pressure-tested and refined; log
  friction points to improve the library before client 4–5
- Begin capturing early wins (even week-2/3 database-growth numbers, not just final revenue) for
  use in outreach and on the Results page as soon as they exist

## Phase 4 — Days 76–90: Proof & Pipeline
- First client approaches or hits their Week 6 launch / start of guarantee measurement
- Second and third clients mid-buildout
- Compile the first real (even if partial/early) data points into a Results page update and a
  first draft case study (`templates/case-study-template.md`) — even directional early numbers
  ("list grew 340% in 6 weeks," "3 automations live driving X bookings") are usable social proof
  before the full 90-day guarantee number is in
- Re-engage every prospect who went quiet in Phase 2 with a short "here's what's happening with
  [Client 1]" update — reintroduces the offer with real proof instead of a cold restart
- **Target by day 90:** 3 signed clients, one at or near its 90-day guarantee checkpoint, a
  refined and battle-tested set of SOPs/templates, and a warmed pipeline of 5–10 additional
  qualified prospects ready for the next sales push

## Plan for landing the first 3 clients specifically

1. **Client 1 — warmest possible target.** A course with an existing relationship (personal
   network, or a course already featured/friendly with Discover Oregon Golf). Offer the pilot.
   The goal here isn't margin, it's a clean, well-executed first engagement to generate the first
   real proof point and testimonial.
2. **Client 2 — a different course type than Client 1** (e.g., if Client 1 is municipal, target a
   semi-private or small resort for Client 2). This deliberately builds case-study range early —
   "works for munis" and "works for semi-private" are both needed to sell broadly across the
   target list.
3. **Client 3 — from cold outreach, not network.** Proves the outbound motion itself works
   independent of relationships, which is the real test of whether this scales past personal
   connections. Use Client 1's early results as social proof in the pitch.

Do not discount below the pilot terms to force a signature — the guarantee itself is the
risk-reversal; further discounting undermines the pricing discipline the whole model depends on.
